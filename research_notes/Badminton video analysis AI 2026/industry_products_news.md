## 1. What have BWF, major tournaments and broadcasters done with AI/analytics in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
BWF's 2025–26 tech moves are about **officiating and production, not player analytics**. It now has five approved Instant Review System (IRS) vendors, two of them new AI/computer-vision entrants (Stupa, Reveal Lens). Its Infront deal (to 2034) promises "data-driven graphics" from 2027. **3×15 scoring was approved and starts 4 Jan 2027.** I found no BWF "AI data partner" and nothing badminton-specific in the Olympic/LA28 AI plans.

### Cited Findings

It **takes effect Monday 4 January 2027** — [BWF](https://bwfbadminton.com/news-single/2026/04/25/bwf-members-approve-3x15-scoring-system); [USA Badminton](https://usabadminton.org/bwf-approves-3x15-scoring-system/)

### Inferences
- **3×15 from 4 Jan 2027 is the single most actionable item for BC Rheinbach.** Any score/format library, rally-to-game segmentation or "game won at X points" logic should be parameterised (target points and setting cap), not hard-coded to 21/30. Footage recorded before and after 2027 will mix formats.
- Every BWF-approved IRS uses **multiple synchronised high-speed cameras** (Reveal Lens: 12 cameras at 240–480 fps). That is strong evidence that reliable 3D shuttle position and speed need multi-view, high-frame-rate capture. A single DJI ultrawide can only produce estimates, which supports keeping the "Schätzung" label.
- Stupa Cast's metric set (serve success, rally count, movement areas, error distribution, winner placement) is a ready-made template for "broadcast-grade" per-player stats from rally and shot data.
- GroundTouch GmbH (Hamburg) is a German BWF-approved IRS vendor and could be a local contact or benchmark source.

## 2. Which consumer/club products analyze badminton video with AI, and what do they offer?

### Takeaway
The 2025–26 consumer market is many **small phone-video apps** plus a few **hardware entrants**. The apps do rally clipping, shot classification, heatmaps, pose-based technique scoring and, increasingly, an **LLM "coach chat" on top of the analysis**. Hardware entrants are mostly Chinese/Taiwanese AI cameras and robots. The big team-sport camera platforms (Pixellot, Veo, Spiideo, PlaySight, Hudl) and SwingVision have **no confirmed badminton product**. Pricing runs from about €3/month to about $30/month, usually as minute-based quotas.

### Cited Findings

Pro plan $28/month or $280/year; the terms also list a $7/month Beta plan and say the service is in beta — [GGAB app](https://app.getgoodatbadminton.com/); [GGAB terms](https://app.getgoodatbadminton.com/terms); [GGAB changelog](https://app.getgoodatbadminton.com/changelog)

A coaching chat remembers your analysis and earlier videos. 12 free minutes; plans from €2.99/month (Essential = 20 min of video per month); the free Starter plan includes 10 coach messages per month — [Peak](https://badmintonpeak.com/en)

### Inferences
- **"Standard" consumer features in 2026** (seen across 3 or more products): (1) automatic rally detection and dead-time removal / highlight cuts; (2) shot-type classification (6 to 28 classes); (3) court heatmaps and positioning; (4) pose-based technique feedback on short clips; (5) an **LLM coach chat grounded in the analysis output** (Peak, GGAB, Badminton Analyst). Pro comparison and "patterns that cost points" (GGAB) is the differentiator at the top end.
- **Shuttle speed is mostly a hardware feature.** It comes from multi-camera high-speed rigs (ITRI, IRS vendors), radar, or watch/racket sensors. Phone apps rarely make speed central, which fits BC Rheinbach's choice to present speed as an estimate.
- Several apps stress **on-device or privacy-first processing** and auto-deletion (RallyCut, RallyShot, Badminton Analyst, 30-day deletion). For a club app holding members' video, an explicit retention policy is a feature worth copying.
- Pricing reference: €2.99 to $28–30 per month, often metered by video minutes. A self-hosted club tool avoids this, but minute quotas also hint at the vendors' real compute costs.
- **Gap in the market:** no product found targets *fixed, permanently mounted club cameras with per-member longitudinal reports* in Europe. SportVision does this in China (hall installations), which makes it the closest analogue to BC Rheinbach's setup.

### Gaps

## 4. Independent reviews or tests of accuracy

### Takeaway
**Almost no independent accuracy testing of consumer badminton AI exists.** The only third-party-validated number is BWF's IRS homologation (Stupa: 99% vs 98% threshold), and even that is relayed through company press releases without a public method. All consumer app accuracy figures (e.g., BadPro+ ">99%") are vendor marketing. Academic and prototype numbers are much lower, especially for shuttle detection, and vision-based speed estimates are known to disagree with radar.

### Cited Findings
- **Stupa IRS**: BWF homologation tests (Sept and Dec sessions) gave 99% accuracy against a 98% benchmark. The figure comes from BWF's letter as quoted in company PR, and the test protocol is not public — [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/stupa-sports-ai-review-system-gets-bwf-certification-for-global-tournaments/); [IT Brief](https://itbrief.in/story/stupa-wins-bwf-approval-for-badminton-review-system)
- **Reveal Lens**: no published accuracy figure. Coverage is mostly Malaysian outlets repeating company statements, and the hardware specs conflict (240 vs 480 fps; 8 vs 12 cameras) — [Revealtek](https://www.revealtek.com/reveal-irs); [SoyaCincau](https://soyacincau.com/2026/08/25/malaysian-ai-badminton-review-system-reveal-lens-antica-singapore-international-challenge/)
- **BadPro+**: ">99% kinetic shot classification accuracy" across 28 shot types is **vendor-only**, with no validation report found — [BadPro+](https://badproplus.com/)
- **MatchMotion** (academic mobile-app prototype, April 2026, YOLOv8): **37.6% mean shuttle detection accuracy** and **81.7% hit recognition**, on 10 BWF World Championship rally videos. Broadcast footage is easier than amateur footage — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404597971_MATCHMOTION_AN_AI-POWERED_MOBILE_APPLICATION_FOR_AUTOMATED_BADMINTON_VIDEO_ANALYSIS_USING_YOLOV8_OBJECT_DETECTION)
- **(earlier, research baselines)** Published monocular systems on BWF video report about 89.7% accuracy and 90.5% F1 for shot detection, and shot-type classification of 72.1% / 65.4% / 54.1% at three thresholds. Which number belongs to which paper was not verified — [Sensors 2024 / PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11244353/); [arXiv 2307.16000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16000)
- **Smash speed, vision vs radar**: a Sept 2025 arXiv paper (Diwen Huang) builds a phone system (custom YOLOv5, Kalman filter, kinematic speed). One search summary says its app "consistently recorded substantially higher speeds than the radar gun", but another could not confirm the paper's comparison numbers. **Treat as unverified.** The paper also notes cheap consumer radars often fail to detect shuttles — [arXiv 2509.05334](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05334v1)
- **(earlier, 2022, peer-reviewed)** Phomsoupha et al. compared a Doppler radar gun and a Zepp motion sensor against high-speed video for smashes. Both were highly reproducible, but **radar underestimated speed by ~9.7% and the sensor by ~13.6%** — [Int. J. Racket Sports Sci.](https://revistaseug.ugr.es/index.php/IJRSS/article/view/33259)
- **ITRI trainer hands-on (CES 2025)**: a Fast Company reporter (not a trained player) measured a shot at about 52 mph (about 84 km/h). This is anecdotal, not a validation. Olympian Howard Shu endorsed the speed data — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91258203/ai-taught-me-to-be-a-slightly-better-badminton-player-at-ces); [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6011088)
- **Wearable stroke detection**: a padel field test of the Huawei Watch Fit 5 Pro found the forehand/backhand split accurate "as far as it goes", with nothing beyond that (padel, not badminton) — [Topics of Tennis](https://www.topicsoftennis.com/i-took-the-huawei-watch-fit-5-pro-onto-a-padel-court-heres-what-the-numbers-said/)
- **Community reviews**: RallyCut (iOS) has 1 rating — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rallycut-ai-badminton-editor/id6752290110). No r/badminton or YouTube head-to-head reviews of AI badminton apps surfaced in search — [search-surfaced listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.incubus.badmintonanalyst)

### Inferences
- **Benchmarks to borrow:** (a) BWF's 98% in/out threshold as the bar for any "line call" claim, which a monocular club camera should not attempt; (b) shuttle detection is the weakest link (37.6% in a 2026 prototype, even on broadcast video). Gating downstream stats on detection confidence and showing coverage ("shuttle seen in X% of frames") would be more honest than any 2026 consumer app.
- For validating BC Rheinbach's speed estimate: high-speed video is the reference standard, radar reads about 10% low, and phone-vision estimates can read high. A quick **in-club validation** (240 fps phone slow-mo of a few smashes vs the pipeline's estimate) would give an independent error bar that no commercial product publishes.
- Since no consumer product publishes per-class confusion matrices, publishing even a small labelled-clip benchmark (e.g., 200 annotated shots from club footage) would set BC Rheinbach above the market on transparency.

### Gaps
- No independent tests were found for GGAB, Peak, BadPro+, SportVision, XbotGo badminton mode, Coollang (2025–26) or Reveal Lens.
- BWF's IRS homologation protocol (what "99% accuracy" measures, sample size, in/out vs landing-point error in mm) is not public in the sources found.
- I could not open the arXiv smash-speed paper's results section (proxy block), so its vision-vs-radar error is unconfirmed.

## 5. Notable open-source end-user projects (GitHub) for amateur badminton analysis

### Inferences
- **Good-Badminton is the closest open-source analogue to BC Rheinbach v2** and the obvious benchmark: same outputs (pose, shuttle, court mapping, heatmaps). It probably lacks per-player longitudinal reports, multi-match aggregation, a DB/web layer and German UX. Its fast star growth (~1k in 4 months) shows strong amateur demand. Comparing its shuttle detector on club footage against BC Rheinbach's stage 5 (white shuttle vs white wall) would be a cheap external baseline.
- The common 2026 hobby stack (YOLO11/YOLOv8 + TrackNetV3 + homography + Streamlit/React dashboard + LLM narrative) matches BC Rheinbach's plan. The differentiators would be calibration quality, honesty about uncertainty, and the club-level data model, not the model choice.
- Licensing: CLAUDE.md allows non-commercial licenses but forbids no-license dependencies. Good-Badminton's **code** license was not verified (only the model asset is reported as Apache-2.0), so check it before borrowing code.

- Good-Badminton's README could not be read directly (the GitHub MCP is restricted to the user's own repo). Feature details come from a search summary, so its code license, model stack and accuracy are unverified.
- Activity is shown by `updated_at` and stars, not commit frequency or issue health. Some "updated" timestamps may only reflect stars or forks.
- No Reddit/forum evidence of which open-source tools amateur clubs actually run.
